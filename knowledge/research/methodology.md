# Research Methodology

Systematic approaches to conducting research ensure quality, reproducibility, and trustworthiness of findings.

## Research Process

1. **Question formulation**: Define clear, specific research questions
2. **Literature review**: Survey existing knowledge and identify gaps
3. **Source discovery**: Find relevant sources across multiple channels
4. **Source evaluation**: Assess quality, relevance, and credibility
5. **Data collection**: Extract and organize information
6. **Analysis**: Identify patterns, themes, and contradictions
7. **Synthesis**: Combine findings into coherent conclusions
8. **Reporting**: Present findings with proper attribution

## Source Evaluation Criteria

### CRAAP Test
- **Currency**: How recent is the information?
- **Relevance**: Does it relate to the research question?
- **Authority**: Who is the source? What are their credentials?
- **Accuracy**: Is the information verifiable? Evidence-based?
- **Purpose**: Why does this information exist? Bias considerations?

### Source Hierarchy
1. Primary sources (original research, data)
2. Secondary sources (analysis, reviews)
3. Tertiary sources (encyclopedias, textbooks)
4. Popular sources (news, blogs)

## Evidence Quality
- **Strong evidence**: Peer-reviewed, replicated findings
- **Moderate evidence**: Well-conducted studies, expert consensus
- **Weak evidence**: Anecdotal, single-source, unverified claims
- **Insufficient**: Claims without supporting data

## Handling Contradictions
When sources disagree:
1. Evaluate the credibility of each source
2. Consider the methodology behind each claim
3. Look for meta-analyses or systematic reviews
4. Note the contradiction explicitly
5. Present both perspectives with appropriate context
